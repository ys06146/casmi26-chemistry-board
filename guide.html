02

Your three tasks

Bring observations, interpretations, and proposals as separate parts of the review.

A

Compare the correct and incorrect structures

In plain English: find where the two molecular assemblies differ.

  • Check molecular formula and connectivity separately. Equal formulas do not establish equal structures.
  • Compare functional groups, their counts and positions, rings, scaffold, side-chain attachment points, and bond orders.
  • Separate genuine connectivity differences from tautomer or representation differences. This evaluation uses the first 14 characters of the canonical-tautomer InChIKey.
  • Tag recurring error patterns and identify the specific atoms or bonds involved.
Deliver: two structure images, 2–4 sentences describing the difference, and error tags. Do not infer positional isomerism from formula equality alone.
B

Look for spectral evidence that distinguishes the candidates

In plain English: explain which fragments fit which structure, and why.

  • Record spectrum ID, precursor m/z, adduct, instrument, and collision energy first.
  • Choose about three informative peaks if possible. If there are not enough discriminating peaks, say so.
  • For each peak: report m/z, intensity, possible composition or pathway, which candidate it supports, and your confidence.
  • For neutral-loss hypotheses, check charge, elemental balance, exact mass, tolerance, and possible rearrangements.
  • Inspect other energies or instruments for the same molecule. Record peaks both candidates can explain, too.
Deliver: an evidence table, a structure-based explanation, and confidence with limitations. A matching mass difference starts the interpretation; it does not finish it.
C

Turn the interpretation into a testable feature

In plain English: specify what a program should score and how we can tell if it helps.

  • Identify the cases and conditions where the clue should apply: adduct, structural motif, energy, and acquisition setting.
  • Define computable inputs and outputs: fragment support, mass error, or consistency across observations.
  • Distinguish chemical discrimination between structures from the model's ranking behavior.
  • Include at least one counterexample. Start weak rules as a small bonus, not an automatic rejection.
Deliver: 2–3 proposals across the full case set, each with evidence, a feature definition, scope, counterexamples, and a controlled comparison.

Who owns what?

Chemistry reviewer

Structure comparison, fragment interpretation, uncertainty, and chemical hypotheses.

Model developer

Extract inputs and scores, trace branches, implement features, run controlled tests, and submit.

Joint decisions

Error taxonomy, counterexamples, new validation split, minimum useful gain, and acceptable cost.

1. Same formula, different connections

Both candidates have formula C₂₇H₃₃N₃O₈ and neutral monoisotopic mass 527.22677 Da. Equal formulas do not make them the same structure or merely positional isomers.

Plain English: They contain the same inventory of atoms, but those atoms are assembled differently. A has a terminal –NH–CH₂–N(pyrrolidine) group attached to a four-ring core. B does not have that same connection, although it also has a nitrogen-containing five-membered ring. We cannot rule out every alternative way for B to produce an ion near 84.

Moderate for the proposed composition/origin; the fragmentation mechanism remains unconfirmed. The candidate's stereochemistry is not established.

Separate the effects of weights and depth; then compare grouping before fusion; then test a small chemical support score.
Counterexample
Other scaffolds might produce the same 84 ion. A weak or absent signal under different collision conditions should not automatically eliminate a candidate.

For each candidate, detect the relevant terminal structural motif and test whether a compatible peak near 84.08078 is observed under appropriate acquisition conditions. 04

What to deliver

Keep the review specific enough to turn into a controlled experiment.

  1. Eleven case records

    Two structures, differences, peak evidence, conditions, uncertainty, branch ranks, a hypothesis, and counterexamples. A well-supported explanation of why a spectrum cannot distinguish the pair is also a useful result.

  2. One summary of recurring errors

    Tag counts, representative IDs, shared patterns, and differences from the comparison cases. Multiple tags per case mean totals can exceed 11.

  3. Two or three experiment proposals

    Define implementable features and an order for testing them separately.

  4. Checks on the six improved cases

    Ask whether a proposed rule would undo v4's gains. This check does not replace evaluation on the full set or fresh molecules.

A one-page experiment proposal

  1. Observed facts: case IDs, structural differences, peaks, and ranks.
  2. Chemical hypothesis: the proposed explanation and confidence.
  3. Computable feature: input fields, justified mass tolerance, output value, and score direction.
  4. Scope and counterexamples: adduct, energy, structural context, and likely failure cases.
  5. Controlled comparison: fix candidate pool, existing weights, and depth; add one feature.
  6. Decision criteria: MRR@25, Top-1, Top-25, subgroup effects, runtime, and confirmation on new molecules.

Joint-review checklist

  • Are observations, interpretations, and proposed experiments clearly separated?
  • Does the evidence discriminate the candidates, or only sound plausible?
  • Are unexplained peaks and counterexamples included?
  • Can weight effects and feature effects be tested separately?
  • Is final confirmation reserved for a separate set of molecules?

05

Concepts to know

Start with the plain-language explanation; expand each item for the level this project needs.

Formula versus structure

A formula counts the ingredients. A structure shows how they are connected.

For this project

Distinguish connectivity changes from tautomers and normalization. The evaluation key is the first 14 characters of the canonical-tautomer InChIKey; stereochemistry is not scored separately.

MS/MS and fragment peaks

A selected precursor ion is fragmented, and the resulting ions are measured by mass-to-charge ratio and relative signal intensity.

For this project

Peak position and intensity are observations. Fragment identity and bond-breaking pathways are interpretations. Several structures may explain one peak. Review the adduct, collision energy, instrument, and uncertainty. National MagLab explanation.

[M+H]+ and collision energy

[M+H]+ is a protonated molecule. Collision energy describes the energy condition used to produce fragments.

For this project

These RIKEN inputs are positive [M+H]+ spectra with observed energies. Keep evidence from each acquisition separate before comparing across conditions. The workspace identifies which observations were used for ICEBERG scoring.

Neutral losses and mass error

An ion can lose an uncharged piece. A mass difference helps suggest the composition of what left.

For this project

Subtract m/z only under appropriate charge assumptions; check elemental balance, exact mass, precision, and rearrangements. Do not apply an arbitrarily narrow ppm tolerance to rounded precursor values or assign a functional group from one peak alone.

Rank fusion and MRR@25

Rank fusion combines model recommendations. MRR rewards correct answers near the front.

For this project

An RRF branch contributes w/(10+r), which is a score, not a probability. MRR@25 averages 1/r for correct ranks 1–25 and zero otherwise. A change from #1 to #2 costs 0.5 for that case, or 0.005 in a 100-molecule mean.

06

Data scope and sources

This guide analyzes existing runs. It does not report a new model run, tuning result, or submission.

  • Truth and observations: confirmation_truth.parquet and validation_queries.parquet.
  • Case selection: baseline and selected ranks in confirmation.json.
  • Saved branches and final ranks: baseline_v3.csv, submission.csv, dreams_top100.csv, and iceberg_scores_top100.jsonl.
  • Drawings and descriptors: RDKit parsing, formula, exact mass, ring counts, and 2D coordinates with native SVG. Official RDKit documentation.
  • Worked-example analysis · Presentation: ranking regressions · Team discussion board.

The original cohort has 100 structures and 279 spectra. This public package includes only the approved subset of 17 structures and 44 spectra. RIKEN-origin references, equivalent structures, and duplicate queries were removed from the search reference set; overlap with model pretraining was not ruled out.

All case data are embedded, so the guide can be downloaded as one HTML file. Public downloads do not contain the full original experiment data or local filesystem paths.
